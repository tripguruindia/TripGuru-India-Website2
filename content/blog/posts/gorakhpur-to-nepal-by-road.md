If you're in Gorakhpur and thinking of driving to Nepal, you're closer than most of India to one of the easiest international borders to cross. The Sunauli–Belahiya crossing is about 90 km away, and from there it's a further few hours on to Pokhara or Kathmandu. No visa, no long immigration queue — just the right ID, a few rupees of patience at the border, and a day's drive.

This guide lays out the actual route, realistic drive times (not the wildly varying numbers you'll get from map apps), what documents you need, whether to take a bus, taxi or your own car, and what things cost as of October 2026.

## The route: Gorakhpur → Sunauli → Kathmandu or Pokhara

From Gorakhpur, NH27 and connecting roads run north to **Sunauli**, the Indian border town in Maharajganj district. The Nepal side of the same crossing is called **Belahiya**, about 3 km from Bhairahawa town, and the two sides share an Integrated Check Post (ICP) with combined customs and immigration counters. Sunauli is the busiest road entry point for Indians travelling into Nepal, well ahead of the other border towns further east or west.

Once you've crossed into Belahiya, the road splits:

- **Towards Pokhara**: via Butwal, climbing into the hills on the Siddhartha Highway.
- **Towards Kathmandu**: via Narayangarh and Mugling, joining the Prithvi Highway for the final stretch into the valley.

If you're travelling by train, the nearest Indian railhead to the border is **Nautanwa**, about 7 km short of Sunauli and now on the broad-gauge line — useful if part of your group is coming in by rail rather than road.

## How far is it, and how long does it take?

Automated map tools give oddly inconsistent numbers for this route — some show 200 km, some over 1,300 km, depending on which roads they route through. The figures below are what travel operators who actually run this route quote, and they match what most travellers report in practice.

| Leg | Distance | Typical drive time |
|---|---|---|
| Gorakhpur → Sunauli border | ~90 km | 2–2.5 hours by car; buses can take up to 4 hours with stops |
| Gorakhpur → Pokhara (via Sunauli, Butwal) | ~280 km | 9–10 hours |
| Gorakhpur → Kathmandu (via Sunauli, Narayangarh, Mugling) | ~330–335 km | 9–10 hours |

That 9–10 hours already includes the border crossing and a couple of stops, but it assumes normal conditions. Add time if you're travelling in the monsoon, after dark, or on a market day when the Butwal or Narayangarh stretches get congested.

A **Gorakhpur–Sunauli expressway is under construction**, which is expected to cut the first leg's travel time once it opens. There's no confirmed completion date yet, so for now, plan around the current road.

## Border formalities at Sunauli–Belahiya: what Indians need

Indian citizens don't need a visa to enter Nepal. What you do need is the right identity document, and this is where travellers trip up most often.

**For adults aged 15–65**, one of the following is accepted:

- A valid Indian **passport**
- A **Voter ID card** issued by the Election Commission of India
- A **photo identity card** issued by the Government of India, a state government, or a UT administration

**Aadhaar card is not valid ID for this age group.** A specific Ministry of Home Affairs rule allows Aadhaar only for travellers **under 15 or over 65**. This catches out a lot of people who assume Aadhaar works everywhere — at Sunauli, for a working-age adult, it doesn't.

A few other things worth knowing before you reach the barrier:

- **Carry originals.** Photocopies or phone photos of your ID are generally not accepted at the border.
- Indian citizens aren't stamped in or out the way other nationalities are — the process is an ID check rather than a full immigration procedure, so it's usually quick once you're at the counter.
- **Border hours aren't fully fixed.** Reports vary on how long the crossing stays open for different kinds of traffic, and the post can be shut for a few days around major holidays like Dashain at the discretion of local authorities. Start your journey early in the day, and if you're relying on a specific crossing time, check locally or with your travel agent before you set off rather than assuming it's open round the clock.

Before you travel, check the latest advisory from India's Ministry of External Affairs ([mea.gov.in](https://www.mea.gov.in)) and the Indian Embassy in Nepal.

## Documents for children and senior citizens

Rules differ a little by age, so it's worth sorting this out before you leave home rather than at the barrier:

- **Under 15, or over 65**: Aadhaar card is accepted.
- **Under about 10**: no passport or Voter ID is required, but carry some photo document that confirms the child's identity and age — a school ID card, PAN card or similar.
- **15–18**: some reports mention a school-issued identity certificate being accepted as a substitute, but this isn't confirmed by a single clear current rule. If you're travelling with teenagers in this bracket, it's safest to carry a passport if you have one, or confirm the current position with us or at the border before travel.

## Taking your own car or bike into Nepal

Driving your own vehicle across is allowed and fairly common from Gorakhpur, but it comes with its own paperwork and time limit.

- At the border, you register your Indian-registered vehicle and get a pass/challan from the Nepali side.
- **Same-day visits** to the nearest Nepali town are free of charge.
- **Overnight or longer stays** attract a fee of roughly **NPR 100–500 per day** depending on the vehicle type (about ₹60–310 at current exchange rates), per the Indian Embassy in Kathmandu.
- There's a cap: an Indian-registered vehicle can stay in Nepal for a **maximum of 30 days in a calendar year**, across all trips combined — it isn't extendable beyond that even if you're willing to pay.
- Carry the vehicle's registration certificate, insurance (check whether your Indian policy extends to Nepal, or buy an add-on before you leave), and your driving licence, along with personal ID as per the age rules above.
- Vehicles turning up without the right paperwork risk being held at the border, so sort this out in advance rather than hoping to manage it on the spot.

Nepal and India are reportedly working on digitising this permit process, but as of now it's still a manual counter process at the border — don't expect an online pass to be ready before you travel.

## Bus, shared jeep, taxi or your own car — which is best?

It depends on your group size, budget and how much you want to manage yourself.

| Option | Roughly | Good for |
|---|---|---|
| AC sleeper/seater bus, Gorakhpur–Kathmandu | from about ₹1,799 one way (fares vary by operator and season) | Solo travellers and budget trips who don't mind a longer, scheduled journey |
| Private AC taxi, Gorakhpur–Sunauli border only | around ₹2,200 | Travellers who want their own transport to the border, then a Nepali taxi/bus onward |
| Private AC sedan, Gorakhpur–Kathmandu, full journey | around ₹11,000 one way, plus tolls and border charges | Families or small groups wanting door-to-door comfort and flexibility |
| Own car | fuel + permit fees + tolls | Those who want to keep the vehicle in Nepal for the whole trip, within the 30-day limit |

These are indicative market rates picked up from booking platforms in October 2026, not fixed tariffs — fares shift with season, fuel prices and demand, so confirm the current fare when you book. For bus schedules and seat availability, check directly with the operator or a booking platform close to your travel date, since timings and routes change.

A private taxi or self-drive car is generally more comfortable for the Gorakhpur–border leg given the number of changes between buses and auto-rickshaws at the crossing itself. Many travellers cross on foot or by a short local vehicle through the ICP and pick up onward transport — taxi or shared jeep — on the Nepal side towards Butwal or Narayangarh.

## Money: what to carry and what not to carry

Nepal has barred the use and possession of Indian currency notes above ₹100 — so your **₹200, ₹500 and ₹2,000 notes are not usable, and technically not allowed to be carried, in Nepal**. Carry ₹100-denomination notes and below, or plan to withdraw/exchange Nepalese Rupees once you're across.

The Nepalese Rupee is pegged at roughly **1 INR = 1.60 NPR**, and this rate has stayed stable through 2026. Money-changers operate on both sides of the Sunauli–Belahiya crossing, and ATMs are available in Bhairahawa and the bigger towns further in, so you don't need to carry large amounts of cash from Gorakhpur.

## Best time to drive this route

The road through Butwal towards Pokhara and through Narayangarh–Mugling towards Kathmandu crosses hill terrain, and the **Narayangarh–Mugling stretch is a recurring landslide spot during the monsoon** (roughly June to September), with blockages reported in past years. This is a structural seasonal risk on this road, not a one-time event — if you're travelling in the monsoon, build in slack time and check current road conditions before setting out, rather than assuming a clear run.

**October to March** is generally the easier window: drier roads, clear mountain views once you're near Pokhara or Kathmandu, and cooler driving conditions. Winter mornings near the border can be foggy, so an early start still helps visibility on the plains stretch.

## A rough day-by-day if you're driving all the way

For a family or small group doing the whole run by road rather than flying into Kathmandu, this is roughly how the days split:

| Day | Plan |
|---|---|
| Day 1 | Leave Gorakhpur early, cross at Sunauli–Belahiya, continue to Pokhara or Kathmandu (9–10 hours total, including the border) |
| Day 2 onward | Sightseeing at your destination — temples, lakes, viewpoints — before the return leg |
| Last day | Retrace the route back through Sunauli to Gorakhpur |

Starting by 5–6 am from Gorakhpur gives you daylight for the hill roads and some buffer for the border formalities, which can take longer on a busy day.
