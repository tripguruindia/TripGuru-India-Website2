Ladakh is the trip where the landscape does the talking. Barren brown mountains, dark blue sky, a river running green through a cold desert, and then a lake that changes colour through the day. It is also a trip where a few rules matter more than anywhere else in India, mainly because of altitude. This guide covers what a first-timer needs: how many days, when to go, how to reach, how to acclimatise, permits, and a Nubra and Pangong plan.

## How many days do you need for Ladakh as a first-timer?

Plan 6–8 days. Fewer than six leaves too little room for acclimatisation, and the rest days are not optional. A 6-day plan covers Leh, Nubra and Pangong. Add a day or two if you want a slower pace or a buffer for weather and road conditions.

Think of the days in three blocks. The first two or three are for settling in at Leh. The middle days are for the high drives to Nubra and Pangong. The last day or two are your buffer, because roads can be delayed and you do not want a missed flight or a rushed last day on a mountain road.

If you can only take five days off work, it is better to pick Leh and Nubra properly than to squeeze in Pangong and spend most of the trip in a car. Families with children or senior citizens should lean towards eight days.

## When is the best time to visit Ladakh?

The usual window is roughly May to October. Here is how the months compare.

| Period | What to expect |
|---|---|
| May–June | Roads start opening. |
| July–August | Peak season with higher prices. |
| September | Clearer and quieter than the peak months. |
| Autumn | Often recommended as the best time. |
| November onwards | Very cold, highways usually close and many stays shut. |

Roads and high passes open and close by season. Check current conditions locally before you set out.

Even in summer, nights are cold and the sun is strong. A warm afternoon in Leh can turn into a chilly evening within an hour, so choose your clothing in layers rather than by the month alone.

## Should you fly or drive to Leh?

Flying is the fastest way, but it gives your body the least time to adjust. You land at about 3,500 m, so your first day has to be rest. Leh is reached by air, or by two seasonal mountain roads: from Srinagar and from Manali. Check airline websites for current flights and fares.

The Srinagar–Leh road is the gentler ascent, while Manali–Leh is higher and tougher. Roads can be blocked by weather or landslides, so keep a spare day. Many first-timers fly in and take a road one way if time allows. That gives you the speed of a flight on one leg and the slow change of scenery on the other. If you travel by train to the road-head, check IRCTC for current trains, and book your onward taxi in advance during peak months.

## How do you acclimatise in Leh?

Ladakh Tourism's advisory says all tourists arriving in Leh must rest at least 48 hours before going to higher-altitude areas. Its suggested sequence is:

1. **Day 1:** complete rest.
2. **Day 2:** short walks around Leh town.
3. **Day 3:** nearby sights at a similar altitude.
4. **Day 4 onwards:** gain no more than about 500 m a day.

The advisory warns that going up too fast above 3,500 m risks HAPE, a serious lung condition. It suggests ascending 300–500 m a day above 3,000 m and drinking 3–4 litres of water daily.

Ask your doctor about medication before you travel, especially if you are a senior citizen or travelling with children, or have a heart or lung condition.

Oxygen is available in portable cylinders at some Leh hotels and pharmacies, and at SNM Hospital.

A practical tip: walk slowly, climb stairs one step at a time, and do not rush to unpack and go out on arrival. If you feel unwell, tell your hotel or driver early and do not push on to a higher place. A slow first day is the cheapest insurance for the rest of the trip.

## Do you need a permit for Nubra and Pangong?

Permit and registration rules for protected areas such as Nubra and Pangong change. Check the official Ladakh Tourism website, or ask us, before you travel.

Most visitors pay an environmental fee and other charges online. The amounts change, so check them on the official Ladakh permit portal before you go. Carry a government photo ID and the printed receipt. The advisory also says not to enter restricted areas without the required permits.

Print the receipt and a few ID copies. Checkposts will ask for them. If you book with an operator or taxi union, ask them what they handle and what you must carry yourself, so nothing is missed at the checkpost.

## What does a first-timer's Nubra and Pangong plan look like?

This is a common operator-style plan for six days. It is not a fixed rule, so adjust it to your arrival time.

| Day | Plan |
|---|---|
| 1–2 | Arrive and rest in Leh. Short walks only. |
| 3 | Local sightseeing: Shanti Stupa, Leh Palace, Thiksey and Shey. |
| 4 | Leh to Nubra over Khardung La, one of the world's highest motorable passes, above 5,000 m. Hunder sand dunes and double-humped camels. |
| 5 | Nubra to Pangong, or return via Leh (subject to road conditions and permits). |
| 6 | Pangong at sunrise, then back to Leh. |

Day 3 is a useful warm-up. The sights are at about the same altitude as your hotel, so you can see Leh Palace and the monasteries without a big climb. Shanti Stupa is a popular spot for looking over the town and the surrounding ranges, and Thiksey is a large monastery complex on a hillside. Opening times differ, so check locally.

Nubra then gives you the high pass and the cold-desert dunes. Spend only a short time at the top of Khardung La, as the air is thin, and keep the walking easy. At Hunder, the sand dunes sit against a backdrop of bare mountains, and the double-humped camels are the local attraction.

At Pangong, the lake is the reward for the long drive: stay overnight if you can, to see it at sunrise. The water colour shifts through the day, and early morning is usually calmer and quieter than midday. Roads and high passes open and close by season. Check current conditions locally before you set out.

## Nubra or Pangong: which is better?

Do both if you have six days. Nubra is greener, with villages, dunes and camels. Pangong is about one large lake and the light on it. If you only have time for one, choose Nubra for variety, and Pangong for the single iconic view.

## What should you pack for Ladakh?

- Layers, and a down jacket.
- Sunscreen, UV sunglasses, lip balm and moisturiser.
- A water bottle, a power bank and your personal medicines.
- Photo ID, the printed fee receipt and ID copies.
- Some cash, as a backup.
- Comfortable walking shoes with good grip, and a light daypack for long drives.

Dry air and strong sun are what many first-timers underestimate, so lip balm and moisturiser are worth carrying even in summer.
