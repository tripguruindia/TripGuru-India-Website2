So you've picked Thailand's Andaman coast, and now the real argument has started — Phuket or Krabi? The honest answer is that they do different jobs. Phuket is the island with the airport, the malls and the nightlife; Krabi is the mainland province next door, with limestone cliffs dropping into jade-green water and a far quieter pace. Most Indian travellers with six nights or more end up doing both, rather than picking one and wondering about the other.

## Phuket and Krabi at a glance

Phuket is Thailand's largest island and its most developed beach destination outside Bangkok. It has its own international airport, big resort chains along Patong, Karon and Kata, shopping malls, pharmacies and private hospitals used to foreign patients — useful if you're travelling with elderly parents or young children.

Krabi is a province on the mainland, not an island. It's built around limestone karst scenery — towering cliffs covered in green, mangrove channels, and beaches like Ao Nang that feel smaller and far less commercial. Phuket's airport is the better-connected gateway from India; most travellers fly into Phuket first and continue to Krabi by road or ferry, rather than flying into Krabi directly. If you're still weighing this trip against other options, our full list of [destinations](/destinations) is worth a browse.

As a rule, Krabi runs noticeably cheaper than Phuket across hotels, food and local boat tours, though the gap narrows once you move into mid-range and upscale properties.

## Beaches: wide and lively, or small and dramatic

Phuket's beaches are long, sandy and set up for a full day out — sunbeds, beach bars, jet-ski operators and restaurants right on the sand. Patong is the busiest and loudest; Kata and Karon are a step calmer; Bang Tao, Surin, Kamala and Nai Thon in the north are quieter still, with a more upmarket, couple-friendly feel and fewer crowds. Bang Tao in particular has grown into a stylish beach-club strip — Catch Beach Club is one of the better-known names here, popular for sunset drinks and daybeds away from Patong's noise.

Krabi's beaches are smaller and framed by the karst cliffs that make this coast instantly recognisable in photographs. Ao Nang is the main base — a working beach town built around a pier for boat tours more than a classic lounging strip. Railay Beach, a short longtail-boat ride from Ao Nang, has no road access at all; it's wedged between cliffs and draws rock climbers and travellers looking for something quieter than Phuket's party beaches. If you want the picture-postcard limestone backdrop with your morning coffee, this is where you get it.

## Island-hopping: Phi Phi, Four Islands and Hong Islands

This is where both destinations shine, just with different signature trips.

From **Phuket**, the big day tour is to the **Phi Phi Islands** — dramatic cliffs, turquoise bays and the beach made famous by *The Beach*. James Bond Island (Ko Tapu) in Phang Nga Bay is the other classic Phuket day trip. Phi Phi's Maya Bay has periodic ecological closures to let the reef recover, so if a Maya Bay stop matters to you, check current status with the tour operator before booking.

From **Krabi**, the standard half-day trip is the **Four Islands tour** — Tup, Chicken Island (Koh Kai), Poda and the beach at Phra Nang Cave — done by longtail or speedboat from Ao Nang Pier, usually with lunch and snorkel gear included. The **Hong Islands** tour, a bit further out, is the quieter, less crowded alternative with calmer water for snorkelling.

Handily, Phi Phi can also be reached as a day tour from Krabi/Ao Nang, which is one reason many itineraries treat it as the link between the two destinations rather than something you must choose between.

## Nightlife: Bangla Road versus bonfire bars

If nightlife is part of why you're going, this comparison decides it for you. **Phuket's Bangla Road in Patong** is Thailand's most intense party strip outside Bangkok — clubs, go-go bars, and a scene that runs well past midnight every night of the week.

**Krabi's nightlife**, mostly around Ao Nang, is a different register entirely — beach bars, fire shows on the sand, reggae bars with live music, and most places winding down by midnight. There's nothing resembling a nightclub scene. If you want to dance till 3am, go to Phuket. If you want a cocktail with your feet in the sand and an early night, Krabi suits you better.

## Families or couples — who suits what

Neither destination is off-limits to either group, but the trade-offs are real.

**Families** generally find Phuket easier: water parks, an aquarium, mini-golf, kids' clubs at many Karon and Kata resorts, and hospitals close by if anything goes wrong. Krabi can absolutely work for families who prefer nature over infrastructure — think gentle kayaking through mangroves, a dip in the Emerald Pool, low-key beach time — but it has far fewer kid-specific attractions.

**Couples and honeymooners** often lean towards Krabi for the quieter, more intimate settings — Railay's cliff-bound beach, Ao Nang's edges away from the main strip. Phuket's northern beaches (Surin, Bang Tao, Kamala, Nai Thon) are a good middle ground if you want the convenience of Phuket's airport and dining scene with a calmer beach to come back to.

These are general patterns from travel guides rather than a hard rule — a food-and-shopping-loving couple might prefer Phuket, and a family that loves rock pools and boat rides might be very happy in Krabi.

## Getting from Phuket to Krabi (and back)

The two are about 165–170 km apart by road. A private car or taxi transfer takes roughly 2.5 hours; shared minivans run through the day, roughly hourly, and take about 2–3 hours depending on stops.

The ferry option runs between Phuket's Rassada Pier and Krabi-side piers (Klong Jilad, Ao Nang, Nopparat Thara or Railay), taking roughly 1.5–2.5 hours depending on the boat. Ferries are more frequent and reliable in the November–April dry season; during the rainy months (roughly June–October) sea crossings can get choppy and are occasionally suspended, so build in a buffer day if you're travelling by boat in that window and have a flight to catch afterwards.

## How to split your nights

There's no single right split — it depends on whether you're chasing nightlife, nature, or a bit of both.

| Total nights | Suggested split | Good for |
|---|---|---|
| 5 nights | 3 Phuket + 2 Krabi | First-timers, short trip, want a taste of both |
| 7 nights | 4 Phuket + 3 Krabi | Balanced, most popular combination |
| 7 nights | 3 Phuket + 4 Krabi | Couples prioritising Railay and the islands |
| 10 nights | 5 Phuket + 5 Krabi | Relaxed pace, time for Phi Phi and the Four Islands both |

A workable rhythm: start in Phuket to land, settle in and do the Phi Phi or James Bond Island day tour, then transfer to Krabi by road or ferry for the quieter back half of the trip, finishing with Railay or the Four Islands before flying home from Phuket.

## Visa, passport and entry rules for Indian travellers

Indian passport holders can currently enter Thailand visa-free for tourism stays of up to 30 days. To use this, you'll need:

- A passport valid for at least 6 months from your date of arrival.
- Confirmed return or onward tickets, hotel bookings and a clear travel itinerary.
- A completed **Thailand Digital Arrival Card (TDAC)**, filled online within 72 hours of arrival at the official site **tdac.immigration.go.th** — it's free, and there are lookalike paid sites to avoid.
- It's advisable to carry at least THB 20,000 per person in cash (or the equivalent) when travelling on the visa exemption.

This exemption is for tourism only — if you're travelling for work, use the correct visa category instead. Thailand requires a passport; Voter ID or Aadhaar are not valid travel documents here, unlike for Nepal. Each family member, including children, needs their own valid passport, and documentation requirements for minors travelling with one parent can have specific conditions, so confirm the current rules before you fly. ## Best time to visit

The dry season, November to April, suits both destinations — seas are calmer for island tours, and December–February is the busiest and most expensive stretch. February tends to be the driest month if you want to time it precisely.

May to October is the rainy season, wettest typically around September–October. Rain usually comes in short, heavy bursts rather than all day, hotel rates can drop by 30–40%, and it's a genuinely good-value window if you don't mind the odd washed-out afternoon or a rougher ferry crossing.

Whichever combination you land on, the comparison really comes down to pace: Phuket gives you convenience, nightlife and family infrastructure; Krabi gives you cliffs, quiet water and a slower few days. Doing both means you don't have to choose.
