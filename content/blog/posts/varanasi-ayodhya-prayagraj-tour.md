Varanasi, Ayodhya and Prayagraj sit within a few hours' drive of each other in eastern Uttar Pradesh, and together they form the most-travelled spiritual circuit in North India. One city gives you the ghats and the eternal flame at Kashi Vishwanath, one gives you the Ram Janmabhoomi and the Saryu, and one gives you the Sangam, where the Ganga and Yamuna visibly meet. Done at an unhurried pace, it rewards early mornings — this guide lays out the distances, a workable day-by-day plan, and the darshan details that shift by season, so you plan around the right timing rather than an old one.

## How the three cities connect

Varanasi, Ayodhya and Prayagraj form a rough triangle, and most travellers drive it in one direction rather than backtracking.

| Leg | Approx. distance | Approx. drive time |
|---|---|---|
| Varanasi ↔ Prayagraj | 120–130 km | 2.5–3 hours |
| Prayagraj ↔ Ayodhya | 160–170 km | 3.5–5 hours |
| Ayodhya ↔ Varanasi | 200 km | 4–5 hours |

A full loop covering all three runs to roughly 600–650 km by road. Most itineraries go Varanasi → Prayagraj → Ayodhya, or the reverse, depending on where you land and where you need to leave from. There's no strong reason to prefer one direction — pick whichever matches your arrival and departure city.

## Getting there from Gorakhpur

Gorakhpur is the natural starting point for travellers from eastern UP and Bihar, and it happens to be the closest of the three cities to Ayodhya.

- **Gorakhpur → Ayodhya**: about 130–140 km via Basti, roughly 3–3.5 hours by road.
- **Gorakhpur → Varanasi**: about 210–225 km, around 4.5–5 hours by road.
- **Gorakhpur → Prayagraj**: about 290–300 km, around 6–6.5 hours by road.

All three cities also sit on the rail network, and Gorakhpur Junction is a major railhead, so trains work for any leg if you'd rather not drive the whole way. Check IRCTC closer to your dates for current trains and timings — we won't quote specific trains here since schedules change.

## Getting there from Delhi

From Delhi, all three cities are well connected by both rail and air — Varanasi and Prayagraj have their own airports, and Ayodhya now has Maharishi Valmiki International Airport as well. Road distance from Delhi runs well over 500 km to Prayagraj and over 650 km to Varanasi, which is long enough that most Delhi travellers fly or take the train rather than self-drive. ## A 5-day circuit that doesn't feel rushed

Three days is enough to tick the big three sites but tight if you want to catch both the morning and evening aartis anywhere. Five to six days lets you breathe.

| Day | Plan |
|---|---|
| 1 | Arrive Varanasi. Evening Ganga Aarti at Dashashwamedh Ghat. |
| 2 | Early Kashi Vishwanath darshan, ghat walk, Sarnath half-day. Overnight Varanasi. |
| 3 | Drive to Prayagraj (2.5–3 hrs). Boat ride to Triveni Sangam, Anand Bhawan. Overnight Prayagraj. |
| 4 | Drive to Ayodhya (3.5–5 hrs). Evening Saryu Aarti at Ram ki Paidi. |
| 5 | Early Ram Mandir darshan, Hanuman Garhi, other temples. Depart or extend. |
| 6 (optional) | Buffer day — useful if darshan queues run long or you want a slower pace, especially for senior citizens in the group. |

If you only have three days, drop Sarnath and keep one city's sightseeing to a single morning — Varanasi or Ayodhya, whichever has the shorter list for your group.

## Varanasi: ghats, Kashi Vishwanath and the Ganga Aarti

Kashi Vishwanath is open from about 3 am to 11 pm, with a pre-dawn Mangala Aarti, a midday Bhog Aarti, and evening aartis from around 7 pm onwards. These timings can shift, especially around festivals, so treat them as a guide and confirm locally or with us before you fix your morning.

The Ganga Aarti at Dashashwamedh Ghat is the evening everyone plans around — priests with brass lamps performing in unison on a platform right at the river's edge, the chanting carrying over the water as boats gather just offshore. It starts a little later in summer (around 6:45 pm) and earlier in winter (around 5:45 pm), and runs about 45 minutes. Watching from the ghat steps is free — get there 30–45 minutes early to find a seat. Boat or platform "reserved seating" offered by private operators nearby runs roughly ₹150–500 per person depending on the season, as of November 2026; that's a boat operator's charge, not an official ticket, so don't pay anyone claiming to sell an "entry ticket" to the aarti itself.

Sarnath, where the Buddha gave his first sermon, is about 30–40 minutes from the city centre and worth a half-day — the Dhamek Stupa, the deer park, and the archaeological museum (open in the daytime — check the exact hours and weekly closing day locally or on the ASI's Sarnath museum page before you go) with a nominal entry fee for Indian nationals.

## Ayodhya: Ram Mandir darshan and the Saryu Aarti

Ram Mandir runs a seasonal schedule that has changed more than once since it opened, and the winter timing in effect from 1 November 2026 has the gates opening at 4 am but general devotee darshan only beginning at 7 am — arriving at dawn doesn't get you in any faster. There are scheduled closures through the day for rituals (around midday for Raj Bhog, for instance) and the temple closes for the night around 10 pm. Because this schedule has moved before and will likely move again with the seasons, check the current timing with us or via the temple trust's own booking portal before you plan your morning around it.

Darshan and aarti passes are free, and can be booked online through the temple trust in advance. Security is tight and reportedly includes restrictions on carrying mobile phones inside the complex — carry as little as possible, and expect lockers for anything you can't take in.

In the evening, head to Ram ki Paidi on the Saryu for the aarti — ghats built in tiers down to the river, lit up as priests perform the ceremony, with a light show sometimes following. Timing here varies by season and source (broadly somewhere in the 5–7 pm window), so ask locally on the day rather than planning precisely around a fixed hour.

Hanuman Garhi and the other temples around the Ram Janmabhoomi complex are easily combined into the same morning or a second day if you have one.

## Prayagraj: Triveni Sangam and Akshaya Vat

The Sangam — where the Ganga and Yamuna meet, with the Saraswati said to join them unseen — is usually visited by boat. A shared boat out to the confluence point for a holy dip typically costs about ₹200–500 per person as of November 2026; bargain before you board, and agree the price for the round trip, not just getting out there.

Anand Bhawan, the Nehru family home turned museum, and the Lete Hanuman Mandir near the Sangam are the other common stops.

One site needs planning ahead: Akshaya Vat, the "immortal banyan tree," sits inside Allahabad Fort, which is under Indian Army control. Outside major mela periods, you can't just walk in — visiting requires prior permission arranged with the fort/army authorities. ## Best time to go

October to March is the comfortable window for all three cities — daytime temperatures around 10–27°C, manageable for the amount of walking and queuing involved in ghat visits and temple darshan. November to February is peak season, so expect bigger crowds and book hotels a little ahead. Summer (April–June) gets uncomfortably hot for pilgrims doing a lot of standing in queues, and the monsoon (July–September) can raise water levels at the ghats and the Sangam, which sometimes affects boat rides — nothing that rules out a monsoon trip, just worth checking conditions locally before you go.

## Darshan tips and practical notes

- Dress modestly for all temples; footwear comes off at the entrance everywhere.
- Expect airport-style security (metal detectors, bag checks) at both Kashi Vishwanath and Ram Mandir — arrive a little early and avoid carrying bags or metal items you don't need.
- This circuit works fine for senior citizens, but build in rest between the ghat walks, boat rides and temple queues rather than stacking everything into one day — the optional sixth day in the itinerary above is there for exactly this.
- No visa or special permit is needed anywhere on this route since it's entirely within India — carry a standard photo ID (Aadhaar, Voter ID, PAN, driving licence or passport) for hotel check-ins, and remember children's ID rules can differ, so check with your hotel if travelling with minors.
- Before you travel, check the latest advisory from India's Ministry of External Affairs ([mea.gov.in](https://www.mea.gov.in)) if your itinerary involves any onward international leg.
