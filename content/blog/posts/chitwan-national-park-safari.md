Chitwan is the one part of a Nepal trip where the plan is built around animals, not temples. You get up before dawn, climb into a jeep or a dugout canoe, and go looking for a one-horned rhino standing in the mist on a riverbank, or a gharial crocodile sunning itself on a sandbar. Most people who build a Nepal itinerary around Kathmandu and Pokhara add Chitwan almost as an afterthought — it deserves better than that, because it sits conveniently between the two and because nowhere else in Nepal gives you this kind of wildlife at this kind of ease.

This guide covers the three main ways to see the park — jeep, canoe and walking safari — which one suits which traveller, when to go, where to stay, and how to fit Chitwan into a Kathmandu–Pokhara circuit without wasting a day on the road.

## Jeep, canoe or walking safari — which should you pick?

Most two- or three-night trips do all three, in a mix spread over a couple of mornings and afternoons, because each one shows you something different.

| Safari type | What it's for | Typical length | Best for |
|---|---|---|---|
| Jeep safari | Covering ground deep inside the core park on fixed forest tracks, with a government-approved guide | Half a day | Rhino sightings (near-certain), the best (though still not guaranteed) chance of a tiger |
| Canoe safari | A dugout canoe drift down the Rapti River | About 45 minutes to an hour | Gharial and mugger crocodiles basking on the banks, kingfishers, herons and egrets |
| Jungle walk | A guided walk through forest and grassland on foot | Half a day to a full day (up to about 8 hours) | A quieter, slower feel for the jungle — birdsong, deer, langurs, and the chance of close-range rhino or sloth bear encounters |

**Jeep safari** is the one most first-time visitors want, because it goes furthest into the park and gives you the best odds of the big sightings — rhinos are seen on the great majority of jeep drives, while a tiger is a genuine bonus that most visitors still don't get, whatever the brochure photos suggest. One thing worth knowing before you book: jeep safaris inside the core park are typically suspended for about three months every monsoon (roughly June/July to September), when flooding and fallen trees make the forest tracks unsafe. Jeep and other activities in the buffer zone and community forests around the park usually continue, so this mainly affects the deep-park drive. Check current conditions with your operator before you travel if you're visiting in that window.

**Canoe safari** is short but memorable — gliding silently down the Rapti at dawn, watching a gharial slide off a sandbank, is a different pace entirely from a jeep. Chitwan holds a significant share of Nepal's remaining gharial crocodile population, so this is one of the better places in the country to see them. It's usually sold as an add-on rather than a half-day activity in itself.

**Jungle walk** is for travellers who want to actually feel the forest rather than watch it go past a jeep window. Two licensed naturalist guides are compulsory, you walk in a group, wear neutral (not bright) clothing, and follow the guide's instructions at all times — if you come face to face with a rhino or a bear, the guides' call on what to do matters more than instinct. No one is allowed inside the park on foot between sunset and sunrise. Walks continue through the monsoon months when jeep safaris are paused, which makes this the main activity if you visit Chitwan in the rains.

A note on elephant safaris: you'll still see them advertised in Sauraha, but Nepal's elephant-safari tradition is currently under active review by courts, conservationists and tourism operators over animal welfare concerns, and the situation could change. We'd suggest sticking to jeep, canoe and walking safaris, and asking your operator for the current position if you're curious about elephant activities.

## Best time to visit Chitwan National Park

The clearest, driest window is **mid-November to early March**: thin undergrowth, good visibility, comfortable daytime temperatures. An annual grass-cutting opens up sightlines in the park in February–March each year.

**October**, right after the monsoon ends, is a good second choice — the jungle is lush and green and rainfall has mostly stopped — but October/November visitor numbers are in fact among the highest of the year at Chitwan, so book ahead rather than assuming it's quiet.

Avoid the heart of monsoon (roughly June to September) if jeep safari deep into the park is your priority — tracks can be waterlogged or blocked by fallen trees, and jeep safari inside the core park is usually suspended for visitor safety during this stretch. It's a good season for birders and for jungle walks, since this is also peak breeding season for much of the park's wildlife.

## How many days do you need?

**Two nights, three days** is the standard package and enough to do one jeep safari, one canoe ride, and either a jungle walk or a visit to a watchtower, plus an evening Tharu stick-dance show in Sauraha village.

**Three nights, four days** gives you a more relaxed pace — a morning and an evening jeep drive rather than just one, time to try all three safari types without rushing, and a buffer if weather or flooding disrupts a day's plan. If wildlife is the main reason for your Nepal trip, this is worth the extra night.

## Where to stay — Sauraha or a buffer-zone resort

**Sauraha**, the village on the park's eastern edge along the Rapti River, is where almost everyone stays. It has guesthouses through to comfortable mid-range resorts, restaurants, and easy access to the park gate, canoe put-in point and jeep pickup. As of October 2026, indicative nightly rates run roughly ₹750–1,250 for budget guesthouses, up to roughly ₹1,500–5,600 for mid-range resorts — these are approximate booking-site ranges, not fixed prices, so confirm the current rate when you book.

A handful of **premium jungle lodges** sit inside or adjacent to the park's buffer zone further west, near Meghauli and Kasara. These are quieter, closer to the core forest, and priced from roughly ₹9,300 a night upward as of October 2026. They suit travellers who want fewer people around and don't mind paying more for the setting.

For most families and first-time visitors, Sauraha is the easier, better-value base — it's where the canoe and jeep points are, and where the evening cultural programme happens.

## How to combine Chitwan with Kathmandu and Pokhara

Chitwan sits almost exactly on the road between Kathmandu and Pokhara, which is why nearly every circuit itinerary runs Kathmandu → Chitwan → Pokhara, or the reverse, rather than treating Chitwan as a separate detour.

- **Kathmandu to Chitwan (Sauraha)**: around 175 km by road, roughly 5–6 hours depending on traffic and road conditions on the Mugling route.
- **Chitwan to Pokhara**: sources differ a little depending on the route taken, putting it at roughly 145–180 km and about 3–5 hours by road.
- **Kathmandu to Pokhara direct** (if you're skipping Chitwan on the way back): about 200 km, typically 6–9 hours by road, sometimes longer, depending on traffic and ongoing highway works — check current conditions before you travel.

Chitwan also has its own small domestic airport at Bharatpur with short flights from Kathmandu, useful if you're tight on time — check current flight schedules and fares directly with the airline or a booking site, since these change often.

If you're coming from Gorakhpur or anywhere in eastern UP, the nearest border crossing is Sunauli–Bhairahawa. From there it's roughly 4–5 hours by road via Butwal and Bharatpur to Sauraha — our [Sunauli border guide](/blog/gorakhpur-to-nepal-by-road) covers that crossing in detail. A practical loop for an India-based traveller looks like: cross at Sunauli, go straight to Chitwan for your safari, then continue to Pokhara and Kathmandu (or the reverse), flying out of Kathmandu at the end. ## What it costs — entry fees and typical package prices

Official park entry fees for Indian nationals (as SAARC-category visitors) are published in Nepali rupees and have stayed at the same level for some years: around NPR 1,000 per person (roughly ₹625) for park entry, with a jeep safari activity fee of around NPR 5,000 per jeep (about ₹3,100), usually split among 4–6 passengers — rates can vary by operator, so confirm before booking. These figures use an exchange rate of about 1 INR ≈ 1.60 NPR as of mid-2026; the rate moves, so treat this as indicative.

Canoe safari doesn't appear to have a separately published government fee — it's generally bundled into a day-tour or package price rather than charged on its own, so don't expect a standalone rate at the gate.

Third-party tour packages covering 2 nights/3 days with accommodation, meals and activities typically range from around ₹15,000–21,000 per person for budget-to-mid-range stays, up to around ₹27,000–28,000 per person where the package includes extra activities, based on operator listings seen in 2026. ## Documents Indians need for Chitwan

Indians don't need a visa to enter Nepal, under the longstanding India–Nepal treaty arrangement. For identity, Indians typically need a valid passport or original Election Commission of India Voter ID card to travel to Nepal; Aadhaar and PAN cards are generally not accepted for adults. Rules for children and senior citizens can differ and have changed before, so confirm the current position with the Indian Embassy in Kathmandu before you travel. This is enforced strictly for air travel; land border posts are sometimes described as more relaxed, but it's safest to carry a passport or original voter ID regardless of how you cross. A printed or photocopied voter ID is not a substitute for the original card.

No separate permit is needed for Chitwan itself — just your park entry ticket and the relevant activity fee, bought at the park counter or through a licensed Sauraha operator.

Before you travel, check the latest advisory from India's Ministry of External Affairs ([mea.gov.in](https://www.mea.gov.in)) and the Indian Embassy in Nepal.

## Safety and what to pack for the jungle

Wear neutral, dull colours — greens, browns, khaki — rather than bright clothing, since bright colours and white stand out to animals. Comfortable closed shoes, a hat, sunscreen, mosquito repellent and a light jacket for early-morning jeep rides (it's genuinely cold in the open jeep before sunrise, even in October) cover most of what you'll need. On a jungle walk, two licensed naturalist guides are compulsory and you must stay with the group and follow instructions immediately if wildlife is encountered at close range — this applies on every walk, whatever the season.

Chitwan is easy to enjoy without overplanning: book your accommodation and activities a few days ahead if you're travelling in October, November or around the Nepali New Year window in April–May when the park is at its busiest, and build in enough nights to try the jeep, the canoe and a walk rather than rushing through on a single morning drive.
