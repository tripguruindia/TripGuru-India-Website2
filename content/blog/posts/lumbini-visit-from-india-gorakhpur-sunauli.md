Lumbini is where the Buddha was born, and from Gorakhpur it is one of the easiest international pilgrimages you can make. You cross at Sunauli, drive into the Nepal plains, and walk through a quiet garden of ruins, a sacred pond and monasteries. Many travellers then carry on to Pokhara.

## How to reach Lumbini from Gorakhpur

The usual route is Gorakhpur → Sunauli → Bhairahawa → Lumbini. Here is how it breaks down.

1. **Gorakhpur to Sunauli:** roughly 90–120 minutes by bus or shared taxi, according to In Your Pocket.
2. **Cross the border:** the Sunauli (India) / Belahiya (Nepal) crossing is a major India–Nepal land crossing. Check border and immigration timings before you travel. Formalities take place on foot or in your vehicle, depending on how you travel.
3. **Border to Lumbini:** about 26 km. A taxi takes roughly 45–60 minutes. Taxi fares from the border to Lumbini vary; agree the fare before you start, or message us for a quote.
4. **By local bus:** go to Bhairahawa first (about 20–22 km from Lumbini), then take a local bus (30–40 minutes) or a taxi (about 20 minutes).

For the border side in detail, see our [Gorakhpur to Nepal by road guide](/blog/gorakhpur-to-nepal-by-road). If you want to take your own car across, message us first, as vehicle rules need checking.

## What ID do Indians need for Lumbini?

Indians do not need a visa for Nepal. For ID, the safest plan is to carry a **valid passport or an original Voter ID with photograph**. Carry originals; we recommend not relying on photocopies or phone copies.

The Indian Embassy in Kathmandu lists a valid passport or original Voter ID as the documents accepted for air travel, and says Aadhaar, PAN and driving licences are not accepted for that. Its page does not cover land borders. Aadhaar alone may not be accepted; carry a passport or Voter ID.

Rules for children and senior citizens differ, so confirm them before you travel.

## What to see in Lumbini

**The Sacred Garden** is the heart of the visit and the UNESCO World Heritage core. It holds:

- the **Maya Devi Temple**, built over the excavated site of the Buddha's birth;
- the **Ashoka Pillar**, the ancient marker of the spot;
- the **Pushkarini sacred pond**;
- the excavated brick ruins around the birthplace.

Check the signs and any staff instructions about photography inside the temple. Go early if you can, when it is quiet and the pilgrims' chanting is the main sound.

Beyond the garden is a zone of monasteries built by different countries; ask locally which are open.

## How does a day in Lumbini unfold?

Most of the day is slow walking, so plan it around energy rather than a checklist. Do the Sacred Garden first, before the heat builds on the plains. Many visitors sit by the pond for a while, and that is part of the experience rather than wasted time. The monasteries fill the rest of the day, each in the style of its country.

Wear shoes that are easy to take off, carry water, and dress modestly, with shoulders and knees covered. These are religious sites, and a cap or umbrella helps with the sun.

## Entry fee and timings

Open hours and fees change, so check them before you go. The Lumbini Development Trust (lumbinidevtrust.gov.np) runs an e-ticket portal and an "Entry Formalities" page. SAARC nationals, which includes Indians, pay a small fee at the Sacred Garden. Confirm the exact amount on the official site. Open in the daytime; check timings locally, as hours can change with the season.

## Day trip or overnight: which is better?

A same-day return from Gorakhpur is possible on paper, but it is tiring. You lose time at the border, and the garden is at its best early in the morning, not at midday. For families, senior citizens and anyone who wants to sit and reflect, we suggest staying one night.

A day-focused plan suits you if you are short of time and travelling with a driver who knows the crossing. Choose an overnight if you are travelling with elderly parents, children or a group, or if you want to continue to Pokhara afterwards.

## How many days do you need in Lumbini?

One full day is enough for the Sacred Garden and a look around the monasteries. A night gets you the garden at first light and takes the pressure off an early Gorakhpur departure.

| Day | Plan |
|---|---|
| Day 1 | Gorakhpur to Sunauli, cross, reach Lumbini, rest, evening walk |
| Day 2 | Sacred Garden early, monasteries, then onward or back |

Carry enough cash for the day, and withdraw in Bhairahawa if you need more. Indian rupees are widely used in Nepal, but rules on higher-denomination notes apply; check the Nepal Rastra Bank (nrb.org.np) or your bank before you travel.

## When should you go?

Summer afternoons on the Terai plains are hot, and the cooler months are easier for walking. Check the weather before booking, and ask us if you want help picking dates.

## Before you travel

Before you travel, check the latest advisory from India's Ministry of External Affairs ([mea.gov.in](https://www.mea.gov.in)) and the Indian Embassy in the country. Also check road conditions between Lumbini and Pokhara, as landslides can happen in the monsoon.
