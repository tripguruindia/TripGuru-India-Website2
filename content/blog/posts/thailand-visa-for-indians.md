As of November 2026, Indians do not need a visa to visit Thailand as tourists for up to 30 days. That is enough for a Phuket and Krabi beach holiday, a Bangkok and Pattaya trip, or both. The limit is 30 days, so ignore blogs that quote 60.

This guide covers the current stay limit, the documents officers may ask for, what to do if you want longer, the mistakes that cause trouble at the counter, and how to use your days well once you are through.

## Do Indians need a visa for Thailand now?

No, not for tourism. Indian ordinary passport holders can enter visa-free for up to 30 days, according to the Royal Thai Embassy in New Delhi. "Visa-free for tourism" means holidays and family visits. Business and work are not covered.

If you plan to stay beyond 30 days, the embassy says to apply for a suitable e-visa at www.thaievisa.go.th before you travel. The visa-free stay cannot be converted into a longer visa after you land, so decide before you fly.

This applies to ordinary passports. If you hold a different type of passport, or you are travelling on a document other than an Indian passport, ask the embassy before you book anything.

## What documents should you carry?

The embassy page confirms the policy, but not every detail below. The list comes from a secondary travel source, so check it against the embassy and official Thai immigration pages before you book.

- **Passport** valid for at least 6 months, with a blank page.
- **Proof of onward travel**: a confirmed ticket out of Thailand within 30 days.
- **Accommodation**: a hotel booking or a Thai address.
- **Proof of funds**: reported as THB 10,000 per person or THB 20,000 per family. Children under 12 are reportedly exempt.
- **Thailand Digital Arrival Card (TDAC)**, filed online at tdac.immigration.go.th. It is reported to be free and to be filed within 72 hours before arrival.

Keep printouts and phone copies. Officers use their discretion on funds and tickets, and entry can be refused if you cannot show them.

For families, carry each child's own passport. Children's rules can differ from adults', so ask the embassy about funds and any other requirement for minors before you travel. If a child travels with only one parent or with grandparents, ask the embassy or the airline what paperwork they advise.

## How does the digital arrival card work?

Fill in the TDAC on the official site before you fly. You will need your passport details, flight details and where you are staying. Use only the official address. Third-party sites may charge for something that is reported to be free. Save the confirmation and keep a copy for the airline check-in and for immigration.

A practical tip for groups: each traveller needs their own details, so sit down together with all the passports and tickets before you start. Do not leave it for the airport queue, where phone signal and patience both run short.

## What does immigration ask on arrival?

Expect simple questions: how long you are staying, where you are staying and when you are leaving. Have your hotel booking, return ticket and some proof of funds ready. Answer plainly and show the papers. Officers may ask to see funds or the ticket, or may not. Be prepared either way.

If you are visiting several places, such as Bangkok first and Phuket later, give the first night's address and be ready to describe the rest of the plan. Keep your hotel confirmations in one folder on your phone so you are not scrolling through messages at the counter.

## Can you stay longer than 30 days?

There are two routes.

| Situation | What to do |
|---|---|
| Planned stay over 30 days | Apply for an e-visa at thaievisa.go.th before travel. Check the current fee there. |
| Already in Thailand, want more time | Apply for an extension at a Thai immigration office. |

An extension is reported at THB 1,900, as of November 2026. At November 2026 rates, that is roughly ₹4,500–5,000. It is granted once, for 30 more days, and approval is not guaranteed. Do not plan a trip on the assumption that it will be approved.

If you know from the start that you want more than a month, for example a long family stay or a work-from-anywhere stretch, the e-visa route is the safer plan. Check the fee and the supporting documents on the official site rather than relying on a blog.

## What mistakes do Indian travellers make?

1. **Planning around 60 days.** Book a ticket out within 30 days.
2. **Booking a one-way ticket.** Without a ticket out, you may be refused boarding or entry.
3. **Skipping the TDAC.** Do it before departure.
4. **Short passport validity.** Check the expiry date and blank pages before booking.
5. **Carrying no proof of funds or hotel.** Have both ready on your phone.
6. **Going for work.** Visa-free entry is for tourism only.
7. **Assuming the stay converts to a longer visa.** It does not.
8. **Relying on unofficial sites.** Use only the embassy, immigration and e-visa addresses given above, and be careful of look-alike sites that charge for free forms.

## How much time do you actually need?

Most Indian trips run 5 to 8 days, well inside the 30-day limit. So the limit is rarely the problem. The real question is how to spread your days so you are not spending them in transit.

| Trip length | A sensible shape |
|---|---|
| 5 days | One base, for example Phuket, with a day trip to the islands |
| 6–7 days | Phuket and Krabi, moving by road or boat between the two |
| 8+ days | Bangkok plus a beach region, with a flight between them |

Check flight options on the airline or a booking site close to your dates, as schedules and fares change.

A beach trip could include the limestone cliffs of the Phi Phi Islands, long-tail boats at Krabi's Phra Nang beach and island hopping from Phuket. Early mornings are the best time to be on the water, before the day boats fill up. Evenings are for beach-side dinners and night markets.

Rules for visas, fees and entry cards change. Check the Royal Thai Embassy New Delhi page, the TDAC site and the e-visa site close to your travel date. The embassy's consular section is on +91 11 2419 7156, Monday to Friday, 9:00 to 17:00.

A simple checklist the week before you fly: passport validity and blank pages, ticket out, hotel confirmations, TDAC confirmation, proof of funds, and travel insurance papers if you have bought cover. Ask your doctor if you need advice on health preparations.
