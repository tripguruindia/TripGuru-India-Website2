Nepal is a short trip from Gorakhpur, but the food feels quite different from what you eat at home. Some of it will be familiar, like dal and rice. Some will be new, like a Newari platter of beaten rice and black beans. This guide covers what to order, where to look in Kathmandu, Bhaktapur and Pokhara, and how to eat well as a vegetarian.

## What are the best Nepali foods to try?

Start with these six. Together they cover the everyday food, the Newar food of the Kathmandu Valley and the food of the mountains.

| Dish | What it is | Veg? |
|---|---|---|
| Momo | Dumplings of Tibetan origin, now treated as Nepali food | Ask for veg |
| Dal bhat | Lentil soup with rice and vegetable tarkari | Usually yes; check ghee and meat sides |
| Newari khaja / samay baji | Platter of beaten rice, lentil pancake, spiced potato, spinach, black beans | Standard version has buffalo meat; ask for veg |
| Thakali thali | Rice, dal and a grain such as buckwheat, millet, barley or maize, with curries | Often veg or non-veg options |
| Sel roti | Sweet rice-flour ring, deep-fried | Yes |
| Juju dhau | "King yoghurt" of Bhaktapur | Yes |

## Is dal bhat really the national food?

In practice, yes. Dal bhat is a staple meal, and it is the staple for trekkers. It is lentil soup with rice, served with vegetable tarkari and usually pickle.

For Indian travellers it is the easiest comfort meal in Nepal. Lentils, rice and vegetables are normally vegetarian, but ask whether ghee is used and whether any meat side dish comes with the plate.

If you are used to a North Indian thali, the plate will feel familiar: a mound of rice, a bowl of dal, one or two vegetable dishes and a pickle on the side.

## What should I know about momo?

Momo came from Tibetan cooking, and Nepal has taken it as its own. You will find steamed, fried and soup versions on almost every street in Kathmandu and Pokhara.

Buffalo ("buff") filling is very common in Kathmandu. If you are vegetarian, say "veg momo" every time and look for a restaurant that cooks vegetarian food separately if that matters to you. Around Boudhanath you can also try thukpa, a Tibetan-style noodle soup that comes spicy or mild.

Momo is a good first order with children, because you can ask for the steamed version and a mild dip. Share a plate across the table and see which style the family likes before ordering more.

## What is a Newari khaja set?

The Newars are the original people of the Kathmandu Valley, and their food is served at celebrations. A khaja or samay baji set is a platter. It usually has chiura (beaten rice), wo (a lentil pancake), spiced potato salad, spinach and black beans.

The standard samay baji includes barbecued buffalo meat, so vegetarians should ask for the vegetarian version. Wo is sometimes topped with egg or meat, so ask about that as well. Chatamari, a rice-flour crepe, is traditionally topped with minced meat and vegetables. Ask whether a vegetarian topping is possible.

For sweets, look for yomari, a steamed rice-flour dumpling filled with sesame, molasses, coconut or sugar.

## Where can I eat Newari food in Kathmandu?

The old Patan and Durbar Square area is a good place to look. Restaurants open and close, so ask your hotel for a current recommendation. For a wider sightseeing plan, see our guide to [places to visit in Kathmandu](/blog/places-to-visit-in-kathmandu).

Meal prices vary a lot between local eateries and tourist restaurants, so check the menu before ordering.

## What is a Thakali thali and where do I try it?

Thakali food comes from the Mustang district. A thali has rice and dal along with a grain such as buckwheat, millet, barley or maize, plus several curries and pickles. It is the more elaborate cousin of everyday dal bhat.

In Kathmandu, ask your hotel for a Thakali restaurant. Check whether you are ordering the veg or non-veg thali.

## What are sel roti and juju dhau?

Sel roti is a sweetened rice-flour ring, deep-fried and flavoured with banana, cloves or cardamom. It is a breakfast and tea-time snack, and a gift at celebrations. Think of it as a cross between a doughnut and a jalebi.

Juju dhau means "king yoghurt" and is a speciality of Bhaktapur. It is made with buffalo milk or cow's milk. It is thick, sweet and usually served in a small clay pot. Try it on a Bhaktapur day trip, near Taumadhi Tole and the old squares, with the pagoda-style temples around you.

## A menu of what to try in the Kathmandu Valley

You do not need a food tour to taste most of this list. Treat the table below as a menu, not a one-day plan.

| What to try | Where to look |
|---|---|
| Sel roti with tea | Local sweet and tea shops near your hotel |
| Thukpa or steamed veg momo | Around Boudhanath |
| Newari khaja set, then yomari if available | Patan Durbar Square area |
| Juju dhau in a clay pot | Taumadhi Tole, Bhaktapur |
| Thakali thali or dal bhat | A Thakali restaurant your hotel suggests, or your hotel restaurant |

Bhaktapur and Patan are separate outings, so do not try to squeeze all of this into one day. Spread them over two or three days of a Kathmandu stay, and the meals will fall naturally between temples and squares.

## How do I eat vegetarian in Nepal?

Nepal is easier than many people expect, but it is not a pure-veg country. Keep to these habits:

1. Say "veg" clearly for momo, thukpa, chatamari and wo.
2. Ask for the vegetarian version of any Newari or Thakali set.
3. Ask about ghee, onion and garlic if you need Jain or no-onion-garlic food. Smaller places may not be able to cook without them, so ask before ordering.
4. Choose dal bhat and sel roti when in doubt.

Tourist areas such as Thamel in Kathmandu and Lakeside in Pokhara have many places used to vegetarian travellers.

## What should I ask before ordering?

A few short questions save a lot of trouble, especially for senior citizens and children. Ask them politely and slowly, and point to the menu line if there is a language gap.

- "Is this veg?" for any dish with a filling or topping.
- "Is there meat in the same pan or the same steamer?" if strict vegetarian food matters to you.
- "Mild, please" if the group does not like heat. Thukpa and dips can come spicy or mild.
- "Is the set served with a meat side?" for dal bhat, Newari and Thakali plates.

If a place cannot answer clearly, choose another one. In a tourist area there is usually a vegetarian-friendly option within a short walk.

## What about sweets, snacks and tea?

Sel roti and yomari are the two sweets most worth looking for. Sel roti is a breakfast and tea-time snack, while yomari is a steamed dumpling with a sesame, molasses, coconut or sugar filling. Both are vegetarian, which makes them easy picks for Indian families.

Festival foods often appear only at certain times of year, so do not plan a trip around finding one. If a shop has it on the day, order it. If not, juju dhau and sel roti are far easier to find.
