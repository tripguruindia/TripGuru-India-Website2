A week is enough to see Rajasthan properly — but not enough to see all of it. In 7 days, most travellers do better picking one of two loops out of Jaipur: the desert route through Jodhpur to Jaisalmer, or the lakes route through Jodhpur to Udaipur. Trying to fit in both Jaisalmer and Udaipur in a single week means two very long driving days back to back, which is doable but tiring, especially with children or older family members along.

This guide lays out both routes day by day, tells you when to go, what a desert camp actually costs, and whether to book a car for the whole week or mix in a train or short flight.

## Jaisalmer or Udaipur — which one fits a 7-day trip?

Jaipur, Jodhpur, Jaisalmer and Udaipur together are often called Rajasthan's "desert triangle," and tour operators love selling all four in one package. The honest answer, backed by how the roads actually run, is that 10–12 days is the comfortable window for all four cities with two nights each. In 7 days, you're choosing between two different trips.

**Go with Jaisalmer if** you want the Thar desert experience — a night in a tent camp at Sam Sand Dunes, a sunset camel or jeep ride over the sand, a living fort you can still walk through at dawn before the shops open. This route suits travellers who haven't done the desert before and are willing to skip the lake city this time.

**Go with Udaipur if** you want palaces, water and architecture — Lake Pichola at sunset, the City Palace, a day trip out to the Jain marble temples at Ranakpur and the hill fort at Kumbhalgarh. This route suits honeymooners, families who want fewer very-long drives, and anyone who'd rather trade the desert night for an extra city.

The one leg that makes both-in-one-week hard is Jaisalmer to Udaipur directly — reports put it anywhere from about 490 to 530 km, 9-plus hours on the road. Most realistic 7-day plans that attempt all four cities route back through Jodhpur rather than drive Jaisalmer–Udaipur directly, which still means two long driving days in a row (Jaisalmer–Jodhpur at roughly 5 hours, then Jodhpur–Udaipur at roughly 4 hr 45 min, often with a Ranakpur stop). It's possible if you don't mind long days in the car, but it isn't restful.

## Two 7-day routes that work

### Route A — the desert loop: Jaipur → Jodhpur → Jaisalmer → Jaipur

| Day | Plan |
|---|---|
| 1 | Arrive Jaipur. Amber Fort, City Palace, Hawa Mahal, Jantar Mantar. Overnight Jaipur. |
| 2 | Drive to Jodhpur (about 335 km, 5.5–6 hours). Evening at leisure in the old blue city. |
| 3 | Jodhpur sightseeing — Mehrangarh Fort, Jaswant Thada, Umaid Bhawan Palace from outside. Afternoon drive towards Jaisalmer or stay overnight and leave early next day. |
| 4 | Drive to Jaisalmer (about 280–285 km, 5–5.5 hours). Explore Jaisalmer Fort and Patwon Ki Haveli in the afternoon. |
| 5 | Morning at Gadisar Lake and the havelis; afternoon drive out to Sam Sand Dunes for a camel or jeep safari, sunset, and an overnight desert camp. |
| 6 | Morning back into Jaisalmer town, or straight on the road back towards Jodhpur/Jaipur (this is a long day — plan a comfortable buffer). |
| 7 | Arrive back in Jaipur (or fly out of Jodhpur/Jaisalmer if seasonal flights are running) and depart. |

### Route B — the lakes loop: Jaipur → Jodhpur → Udaipur → Jaipur

| Day | Plan |
|---|---|
| 1 | Arrive Jaipur. Amber Fort, City Palace, Hawa Mahal. Overnight Jaipur. |
| 2 | Drive to Jodhpur (about 335 km, 5.5–6 hours). |
| 3 | Jodhpur sightseeing — Mehrangarh Fort, Jaswant Thada, the old city. Overnight Jodhpur. |
| 4 | Drive to Udaipur (about 265 km, 4 hr 45 min), ideally routing via Ranakpur so you see the marble Jain temple en route. |
| 5 | Udaipur city — City Palace, a boat ride on Lake Pichola, Jagdish Temple, sunset at the Monsoon Palace viewpoint. |
| 6 | Day trip to Kumbhalgarh Fort (about 85 km, 2–2.5 hours each way; pair it with Ranakpur if you didn't stop there on day 4 — the two are about 45 minutes apart). |
| 7 | Drive or fly back to Jaipur for departure, or fly out of Udaipur directly if your onward journey allows it. |

Both routes anchor on Jaipur because it has the best flight and train connections of the four cities, so most travellers start and end there even if they fly out of Jodhpur or Udaipur instead of driving the whole way back.

## How to reach Rajasthan from Gorakhpur, Delhi or Noida

Jaipur is the easiest gateway into this circuit — it's well connected by air from Delhi and other metros, and by train from Delhi (around 4.5 hours on the faster daytime services). For current timings and fares, check [IRCTC](https://www.irctc.co.in) or your airline rather than relying on a fixed schedule, since trains and flight timings change.

If you're starting from Gorakhpur, the practical route is usually via Delhi: overnight trains run from Gorakhpur to Delhi (roughly 13–15 hours depending on the train), and from there you connect onward to Jaipur by a short flight or a daytime train. Direct Gorakhpur–Jaipur trains exist but are very limited — most travellers from eastern UP will find it faster and easier to break the journey at Delhi.

Within the circuit itself:

- Jodhpur airport has regular flights to Delhi and other cities — check current airline schedules for frequency.
- Udaipur's Maharana Pratap Airport is well connected by air to Delhi, Mumbai and other major cities — check current airline schedules for which routes run non-stop in your travel month.
- **Jaisalmer's airport runs only in the cooler months — roughly October to March.** Outside that window it's effectively closed because of the summer heat and lower demand, so you reach Jaisalmer by road or rail via Jodhpur instead. Always check current flight schedules before planning around it.

## Train vs car for getting between the cities

Inside Rajasthan, most 7-day itineraries are built around a car with a driver rather than trains, simply because the sightseeing stops (forts on hilltops, desert dunes, the Ranakpur-Kumbhalgarh day trip) aren't well served by rail timetables. A private car also lets you stop at roadside dhabas, small step-wells and viewpoints that don't appear on any train route.

Trains and flights make sense for the long single legs — Delhi to Jaipur, or Jaipur to Jodhpur if you'd rather skip a day of driving — but once you're moving between Jaipur, Jodhpur, Jaisalmer and Udaipur, a car gives you more control over an already tight week.

## Desert camps at Sam Dunes — what they cost and what's included

An overnight at the dunes is the one experience Route A is built around: you reach Sam Sand Dunes in the late afternoon, ride out by camel or jeep to watch the sun go down over the sand, eat dinner at camp — often with a folk music and dance performance — and sleep in a tent before an early desert sunrise.

Camp prices vary a great deal by season and property. As researched in October 2026:

- Budget tented camps: roughly ₹1,800–2,900 a night.
- Mid-range camps with better amenities (some with a pool): roughly ₹5,500–7,500 a night in peak winter.
- Luxury tented suites at the high end: ₹30,000 and well upward per night.

Safari activities are usually priced separately from the tent — a half-day camel-and-sunset safari, a longer jeep safari with dinner and folk dance, or a simple camel ride alone, roughly ₹1,500–6,000 per person depending on what's included. Local operators in Jaisalmer town often quote lower rupee prices for Indian travellers who book directly or negotiate on the spot, compared with the dollar-denominated rates you'll see on some international booking sites. Sam Sand Dunes itself has no entry fee; if your plans take you to the nearby Desert National Park, it charges a modest entry fee for Indian visitors — confirm the current amount with the park/forest department before you go. ## Best months for this trip

Winter — roughly October through February — is the season nearly everyone recommends, and it's the only window when Jaisalmer's airport is running if you want to fly in. Daytime temperatures are comfortable for climbing forts and sitting out at a desert camp, though January nights in the desert can get genuinely cold, so pack layers.

| Month | What to expect |
|---|---|
| October | Warming down from monsoon, 20–30°C, good for a desert camp |
| November–December | Pleasant days (15–28°C), cold nights, Rajasthan's busiest tourist season |
| January | Coldest month, 5–22°C, desert nights can be sharply cold |
| February | Mild again, 10–25°C, good all-round month |
| March | Warming fast, 25–35°C |
| April–June | Genuinely extreme heat — Jaisalmer and Jodhpur can cross 40–45°C; plan around the midday hours and check conditions if travelling with young children or senior citizens |
| July–September | Monsoon in eastern Rajasthan (Jaipur, Udaipur) brings occasional heavy showers and humidity, but the Thar desert around Jaisalmer usually stays drier; it's a genuine low-season option with fewer crowds and lower prices |

## What a week in Rajasthan costs, roughly

Based on multiple operator and travel-blog estimates (not a single fixed source, so treat this as indicative), per person for 7 days:

- Budget: roughly ₹20,000–30,000 — shared cab, budget guesthouses, simple meals.
- Mid-range: roughly ₹30,000–50,000 — 3–4 star hotels, a private car, restaurant meals, one desert camp night.
- Luxury: ₹50,000 and upward — heritage or palace hotels, premium desert tented suites.

Fort and monument entry for Indian nationals is modest. Mehrangarh in Jodhpur charges about ₹100–200 for Indian nationals, per [the fort's official site](https://www.mehrangarh.org). Amber Fort in Jaipur and City Palace Udaipur also charge modest entry fees for Indians — City Palace runs a multi-ticket system (palace compound, museum, crystal gallery and vintage car collection are priced separately) — so check the official counter on the day for current rates rather than relying on a fixed number, since these do get revised. ## Documents you'll need

This is domestic travel within India, so there's no visa or passport requirement. A standard government photo ID — Aadhaar, voter ID, driving licence or PAN — is generally enough for hotel check-in, and airlines will ask for a government photo ID at the airport if you're flying any leg of the trip.
