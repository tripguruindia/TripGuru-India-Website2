Bandipur is a small, preserved hill town in Nepal, mostly Newar, sitting between Kathmandu and Pokhara. Most travellers on the Kathmandu–Pokhara road pass its turn-off at Dumre without knowing it is there. Those who climb up find old Newari-style houses, quiet streets and a viewpoint where the snow peaks show at sunrise.

## Why is Bandipur worth a night?

Bandipur was a stop on the Tibet–India trade route, and that history is why its architecture has been preserved. Today it feels like a slower, older Nepal than the highway towns below it.

It suits families, couples and senior travellers who would rather break a long drive than do it in one push. It is also a good contrast after the temples and crowds of Kathmandu (see our guide to [places to visit in Kathmandu](/blog/places-to-visit-in-kathmandu)).

The town is small, so you do not need a list of sights. The pleasure is in walking the bazaar in the evening, having coffee, getting up early for the view and moving on without hurry. If your plan is packed with big-name stops, Bandipur is the day that lets everyone rest.

## Where is Bandipur and how far is it?

Bandipur is on the Kathmandu–Pokhara route, a short climb off the highway at Dumre.

| From | Distance |
|---|---|
| Kathmandu | About 143 km |
| Dumre, on the Prithvi Highway | A short, steep drive up the hill |

The drive from Kathmandu takes about 4–5 hours, depending on traffic and road conditions. Add time for tea stops and the climb from Dumre, especially if you travel with elderly parents or small children.

## How to reach Bandipur

There are two common ways.

1. **Tourist bus.** Buses between Kathmandu and Pokhara will stop at Dumre on request. From Dumre, take a taxi up the hill, or a transfer arranged by your hotel. Most places can organise one if you book in advance, for an extra fee.
2. **Private car.** The road follows the Trisuli River and passes terraced countryside. This is the easier option for families with luggage.

Delays can come from collisions or landslides on the Prithvi Highway, which is prone to them in the monsoon. Check current road conditions before setting out. If you are driving up from the border, our [Gorakhpur to Nepal by road guide](/blog/gorakhpur-to-nepal-by-road) covers the Sunauli crossing.

A few practical points help whichever way you travel:

- Tell the bus staff at the start that you want to get off at Dumre, and confirm it again later in the journey.
- Fix the hill transfer with your hotel before you arrive, so you are not looking for a taxi at the roadside with bags in hand.
- Pack light. The hill road is steep and you will be glad of a smaller bag for one night.
- Ask the hotel how far the rooms are from where the vehicle stops, and whether vehicles can enter the bazaar.

## What to see in the old bazaar

The main bazaar is the heart of the town. Look for old Newari-style houses with wooden windows, quiet streets, small shops, cafes and good spots for photographs.

Stop at a small cafe for snacks and coffee. Newari dishes such as yomari and samay baji are mentioned by visitors, so ask what is available that day.

Go twice if you can: once in the late afternoon when you arrive, and once after dinner or early next morning when the streets are quieter. The same lane looks different when it is empty, and the wooden fronts are best photographed in soft light. Shops are small, so carry some cash in Nepali rupees and do not assume every place takes cards or UPI.

## Where to watch sunrise: Tundikhel or Thani Mai?

**Tundikhel** is an open area in the town with sunrise views over the snow-capped mountains. It is the easy choice, close to where you stay.

**Thani Mai temple** on the hill above the town is a popular sunrise and sunset spot. Ask your hotel about the walk, and take a torch if you go before dawn. Clouds can hide the peaks on any given morning, so nobody can promise the view.

How to choose: if you are travelling with children, parents or anyone who does not like steep paths in the dark, pick Tundikhel. If you are comfortable with an uphill walk and want a wider panorama, go to Thani Mai. You can also do Tundikhel for sunrise and keep Thani Mai for the evening, if you have a second night.

## What is Siddha Gufa like?

Siddha Gufa, the Siddha cave, is described as Nepal's largest. Sources quote different dimensions, so do not rely on one figure. You enter through a narrow opening into a large vaulted chamber with stalactites and stalagmites.

- Bring a torch or headlamp and sturdy footwear.
- A local guide is recommended.
- Check the walking time, entry fee and timings locally.

Ask your hotel how long the visit takes from the town and whether the path suits your group. If someone has knee trouble, breathing problems or a fear of enclosed spaces, they can skip the cave and enjoy the bazaar instead. Wear shoes with grip, not sandals, and keep phones and cameras secure.

## Where to stay in Bandipur

The Old Bandipur area is recommended for heritage buildings and views. Options range from basic guesthouses to boutique heritage hotels. Check current prices locally or message us for a quote.

When you ask a hotel, find out these things:

- Whether the room has a mountain view or looks onto the bazaar.
- Whether they arrange the pick-up from Dumre and what it costs.
- Whether they can arrange a guide for the cave and a wake-up call for sunrise.
- Whether dinner and breakfast are served at the property, as the town is small and options after dark may be limited.

## When should you go?

Views of the snow peaks depend on clear skies, and no one can promise them on a given day. Hill roads are more likely to be affected by landslides in the monsoon, so check conditions before setting out. | When | What |
|---|---|

| Day 1, evening | Walk the bazaar, coffee at a cafe, dinner with Newari dishes |
| Day 2, early | Sunrise at Tundikhel, or the walk up to Thani Mai |

## What should you pack for Bandipur?

- A torch or headlamp for the pre-dawn walk and the cave.
- Shoes with grip for steep lanes and the cave path.
- A light jacket or shawl for early mornings.
- Some Nepali rupee cash for cafes and small shops.
- A small day bag, so the main suitcase can stay in the room or the car.
- Any regular medicines, as pharmacies in a small hill town may be limited. Consult a doctor before travel if you have a health condition.

## Documents and practical advice

Indians do not need a visa for Nepal. Carry a valid passport or Voter ID, and check the current list of accepted documents, including those for children, with the Indian Embassy in Kathmandu before you travel. If you bring a private Indian car, confirm the vehicle paperwork at Sunauli beforehand.

Before you travel, check the latest advisory from India's Ministry of External Affairs ([mea.gov.in](https://www.mea.gov.in)) and the Indian Embassy in the country.
