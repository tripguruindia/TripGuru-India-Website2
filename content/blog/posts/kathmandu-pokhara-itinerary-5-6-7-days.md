Kathmandu and Pokhara make a good pairing. One is a valley of old temples, squares and bazaars. The other is a lakeside town where the Annapurna range shows up over the water on a clear morning. Below are three versions of the trip, followed by how to travel between the two cities and what to leave out.

## How many days do you need for Kathmandu and Pokhara?

Five days is the practical minimum: two nights in Kathmandu, two in Pokhara, with flights between the cities. Six days gives you one more valley stop. Seven to eight days lets you add a road journey with a stop at Bandipur or Chitwan.

If you have fewer than five days, do not try to cover both cities by road. The bus alone can eat a full day.

The two cities feel very different, and that is the point of combining them. Kathmandu is dense and busy: narrow lanes, temple bells, shrines at street corners and bazaars selling everything from brass to prayer flags. Pokhara is slower. Mornings are for the lake, and the evenings are for a walk along the lakeside road. Most people find that two nights in each lets them enjoy both without rushing.

## Should you fly or take the bus from Kathmandu to Pokhara?

The road is about 200 km along the Prithvi Highway, which follows the Trishuli River gorge past hill towns and terraced farmland. Allow roughly 7–10 hours by road, longer in the monsoon. Road works, festivals and traffic can add more. Tourist buses leave in the morning. Check the departure point and time with the operator.

The flight is a short one. Mountain weather is unpredictable, and delays are common in the monsoon (June–September). Do not book a same-day international connection after the flight. For current fares and schedules, check the airline.

| Option | Time | Best for |
|---|---|---|
| Flight | Short | Short trips, seniors, families with children |
| Local bus | Roughly 7–10 hours, longer in the monsoon | Tight budgets only |
| Deluxe tourist bus | Similar | Budget travellers with time |
| VIP sofa bus | Similar | Comfort on a budget |

## What does a 5-day Kathmandu Pokhara itinerary look like?

This follows the shape of common operator itineraries, with two nights in each city.

| Day | Plan |
|---|---|
| 1 | Arrive in Kathmandu. Visit Swayambhunath, the hilltop stupa with views over the city. |
| 2 | Durbar Square in the morning, then Bhaktapur, the old brick town of the valley. |
| 3 | Fly to Pokhara. Spend the afternoon at Phewa Lake, on a paddleboat or kayak, with Machhapuchhre in view. |
| 4 | Shanti Stupa (World Peace Pagoda) above the lake. Add paragliding or a short hike if the weather suits. |
| 5 | Fly back to Kathmandu. If time remains, shop for handicrafts in Patan. |

Day 5 is tight if your onward travel is the same day. Keep the last day light and avoid close connections.

## What is each stop like?

**Swayambhunath.** The white stupa sits on a hill above the city, with painted eyes looking out from the top. Expect a climb of steps, prayer wheels, and monkeys around the complex, so keep food and sunglasses out of reach. The view over the valley is the reward.

**Durbar Square and Bhaktapur.** Durbar Square is a crowd of temples with carved wooden windows and pagoda roofs. Bhaktapur is quieter and built largely of brick, with lanes you can wander at your own pace. Give it unhurried time rather than a quick stop.

**Phewa Lake.** The lake is the centre of Pokhara. Paddleboats and kayaks go out from the lakeside, and on a clear day Machhapuchhre appears over the water. Even if you do nothing else, an hour here in the early morning is worth the wake-up.

**Shanti Stupa.** The World Peace Pagoda stands on a ridge above the lake. It is a good place to look back over the water and the town, and a natural fit for the day you also try paragliding or a short walk.

## What does a 6-day plan add?

The sixth day gives the Kathmandu end more room. You can use it in one of two ways.

- **Patan:** a slower day among its courtyards, temples and craft shops, instead of squeezing it into a departure day.
- **Nagarkot:** a hill viewpoint outside the valley. It suits an early start for the mountain view, if the sky is clear.

| Day | Plan |
|---|---|
| 1 | Arrive in Kathmandu. Swayambhunath. |
| 2 | Durbar Square, then Bhaktapur. |
| 3 | Patan at an easy pace, or an early Nagarkot trip. |
| 4 | Fly to Pokhara. Phewa Lake in the afternoon. |
| 5 | Shanti Stupa, plus paragliding or a short hike if the weather suits. |
| 6 | Fly back to Kathmandu and head home, keeping the day light. |

Keep a buffer on Day 6 for flight delays. For more on the valley's temples and squares, read our guide to [places to visit in Kathmandu](/blog/places-to-visit-in-kathmandu).

## What does a 7–8 day plan look like with Chitwan or Bandipur?

There are two good ways to use the extra days.

**Option A: a road-based loop with Chitwan (7–8 days).** A common tour-operator shape is Kathmandu for 2 days, a drive of a few hours to Chitwan, 2 days of safari, another drive of a few hours on to Pokhara, 2 days in Pokhara, then a flight back. With travel time counted, plan on 7–8 days. It works, but it is a lot of travelling. See our [Chitwan safari guide](/blog/chitwan-national-park-safari) for jeep, canoe and walking options.

**Option B: Bandipur on the way.** Take a private car from Kathmandu and stop at Bandipur, a hill town with an old bazaar, for a night. Then continue to Pokhara. Our [Bandipur guide](/blog/bandipur-nepal-old-bazaar-sunrise-cave) covers the sunrise and the cave.

## What should you skip on a short trip?

- **Chitwan in 5 days.** It needs a long road day each way and eats your Pokhara time.
- **Long road days in general.** If you only have five or six days, fly between the cities.
- **Lesser Kathmandu temples.** Pick Swayambhunath, Durbar Square and Bhaktapur first. Add others only if time is left.
- **Lower-priority Pokhara sights.** The lake, Shanti Stupa and one activity are enough for two nights.

## How should you pace the days?

A few habits make this trip easier, whatever the length.

- **Start early.** Mountain views are usually clearer in the morning, and sightseeing before the day gets busy is more pleasant.
- **Keep one flexible slot.** Weather can cancel paragliding or delay a flight. A free afternoon lets you absorb that.
- **Carry layers and comfortable shoes.** Temple steps, cobbled squares and hill walks are all part of the trip.
- **Dress modestly at temples.** Carry something to cover shoulders and knees, and be ready to remove shoes where asked.
- **Pack light for the flight.** Domestic flights have baggage limits, so check with the airline before you pack.

## When is the best time to go?

Mountain views depend on clear skies, and they are generally more likely after the monsoon. The monsoon (June–September) also brings flight delays. Message us with your dates and we'll advise on the season.

## What documents do Indians need for Nepal?

Indians do not need a visa for Nepal. Carry a valid passport or an original photo Voter ID, and check the Indian Embassy in Kathmandu ([indembkathmandu.gov.in](https://www.indembkathmandu.gov.in)) for the ID rules that apply to your mode of travel and to children.
