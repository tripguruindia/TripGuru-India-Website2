The Andamans are best seen as a short island-hopping loop: a night in Port Blair, then Havelock for the beaches and diving, then quiet Neil, and back to Port Blair for the flight home. The loop works because each island has a different pace, and the ferries between them are short. Here is how to plan it.

Prices and rules here are indicative; confirm them with us or the official Andaman & Nicobar tourism pages before booking.

## How many days do you need for the Andamans?

Five to six days is the usual answer. A 5N/6D plan splits like this:

| Nights | Place | What it is for |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Port Blair | Arrival, rest |
| 2 | Havelock (Swaraj Dweep) | Beaches, scuba, snorkelling |
| 1 | Neil (Shaheed Dweep) | Slow, small-island evening and morning |
| 1 | Port Blair | Sights and a buffer before your flight |

That gives three ferry crossings between overnight stops. The last night in Port Blair is a sensible cushion, though it is not a guarantee against disruption. Most visitors arrive and leave through Port Blair.

## What does a day-by-day Andaman plan look like?

Treat this as a shape, not a fixed schedule. Ferry timings vary, so the order of activities on travel days depends on your booking.

| Day | Where | Plan |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Arrive Port Blair | Check in, rest, short local sights if time allows (night in Port Blair) |
| 2 | Ferry to Havelock | Settle in, an easy beach evening (night in Havelock) |
| 3 | Havelock | A dive or snorkel in the morning, free afternoon (night in Havelock) |
| 4 | Ferry to Neil | Small-island pace, shallow-water dives if you want one (night in Neil) |
| 5 | Neil, then back to Port Blair | Morning on Neil, then travel back to Port Blair (check the ferry route and connections with us); keep a buffer (night in Port Blair) |
| 6 | Port Blair | Port Blair sights such as Ross Island and North Bay; check timings locally, then fly home |

Days 1, 2 and 5 are partly travel. If you want proper time at the beach, add a night at Havelock rather than squeezing in more sights.

## What is the best time to visit the Andamans?

October to May is the best season for scuba diving and underwater visibility. If your priority is the sea, plan inside this window, and ask us before booking outside these months.

If you are travelling with children or elders, pick dates inside the diving season and keep flexibility. Sea conditions can affect ferries at any time of year, so avoid tight connections.

## Do Indians need a permit for the Andamans?

Indian citizens do not need a permit for the main tourist islands such as Port Blair, Havelock and Neil. Check the official Andaman & Nicobar administration website (andaman.gov.in) before you travel.

You do need a valid government photo ID. Aadhaar, Voter ID, driving licence and passport are listed as accepted. Children can use a birth certificate or school ID. Rules can change, so check before you travel.

Carry the original ID for every traveller in the group, and keep a copy on your phone. You will be asked for it at the airport, and ferry operators also ask for ID details at booking and boarding.

## How do the ferries work between the islands?

Private AC ferries connect Port Blair and Havelock, and Havelock and Neil. Check the operators for current schedules and crossing times.

Ferry fares vary by operator, class and season; check the operators or message us for current fares. Book early in peak season, since popular classes sell out. Keep your ferry tickets and ID handy, and keep a buffer before any flight.

A few practical habits help on ferry days:

1. Book ferries before you finalise hotels, so your hotel nights match your crossings.
2. Choose the class by comfort and seating, and ask what the fare includes.
3. Carry a light bag for the crossing and keep medicines and documents with you.
4. Do not plan a dive or a sight straight after landing. Leave room for delays.

## What is there to do in Port Blair, Havelock and Neil?

**Port Blair** is your base on arrival. Ross Island and North Bay are among the Port Blair sights. Official fees and timings were not in our research, so check locally, as some sites close one day a week.

**Havelock** is where most people spend the longest. It is the main diving base, with sites such as Nemo Reef, Dixon's Pinnacle, White Tip Shark and Turtle Beach.

**Neil** is smaller and slower. Its dive sites include Junction, K-Rock and Margarita's Mischief, and it is described as shallow and beginner-friendly.

**Baratang** (limestone caves) is reached by a timed convoy through a protected tribal reserve, with no stopping and no photography. Baratang trips depend on conditions; ask us for the current position.

Jarawa and Sentinelese areas are off limits. Tribal reserves are not sights.

## Havelock or Neil: which suits you?

Choose by the kind of days you want, not by the number of attractions.

| | Havelock | Neil |
|---|---|---|
| Character | Main diving base, the longer stay | Smaller and slower |
| Dive sites | Nemo Reef, Dixon's Pinnacle, White Tip Shark, Turtle Beach | Junction, K-Rock, Margarita's Mischief |
| Good for | Divers, snorkellers, beach days | A quiet evening and morning, shallow beginner dives |
| Suggested nights | 2 | 1 |

Couples and honeymooners often like the contrast: Havelock for activity, Neil for slowing down. Families with young children should think about the ferry crossings and keep the island changes few. Friends' groups who want to dive can give Havelock an extra night.

## How does scuba diving work for beginners?

Scuba is offered at Havelock and Neil, and Port Blair has some sites too. Beginners need no certification and no swimming experience for the introductory programmes. You get a briefing and dive with an instructor.

Beginner dive prices vary by operator and by shore or boat dive; ask us for a current quote.

Consult a doctor before diving, and ask the operator about eligibility. For a first dive, Neil is described as shallow and beginner-friendly, while the operator guide recommends Havelock for most divers.

Before paying, ask what the price includes, how many people are in each instructor's group, and what happens if the sea is too rough on the day.

## What should you ask before booking an Andaman package?

Package prices depend on dates, room type, ferry class and activities, so two quotes can look very different. Ask these questions to compare like with like:

- Which ferry operator and class are included for each crossing?
- Are airport and ferry-jetty transfers included?
- Which hotel area on each island, and how far from the jetty?
- Is any scuba or snorkelling included, or is it extra?
- What is the plan if a ferry is cancelled or delayed?
- Is there a buffer night before the flight home?

## What rules should you follow on the islands?

Rules protect coral and shells; follow local guidance and carry a reusable bottle and cloth bag.
