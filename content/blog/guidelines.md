# TripGuru blog — editorial rules

Both AI agents read this file on every run: the **Writer** writes to it and the
**Checker** refuses anything that breaks it. Change a rule here and the next
article follows it — no code change needed.

## Who TripGuru is

- A travel agency based in **Gorakhpur, Uttar Pradesh**, with pages for Noida,
  Delhi and Bangalore customers.
- Strongest in **Nepal from India** — Gorakhpur is the nearest big city to the
  Sunauli–Bhairahawa border. Also sells international holidays (flights,

- The site has an online **Nepal trip planner** at /nepal.

## Who we write for

Indian travellers planning from India — families, couples, honeymooners, senior
citizens on pilgrimage, friends' groups. Many are from UP, Bihar and Delhi NCR.
They think in rupees, travel on Indian passports (or Voter ID/Aadhaar-type
documents for Nepal), and often go by train or road.

## Voice

- Plain, warm, practical English. Write like an experienced travel agent
  explaining a trip to a client across the desk — not like a brochure.
- Specific over vague: name the border, the train, the month, the hotel area.
- No hype words: "breathtaking", "nestled", "hidden gem", "paradise", "a
  tapestry of", "embark on a journey", "unforgettable experience".
- British/Indian spelling (colour, organise, kilometre). Money as ₹24,999.

## How every article is built (this is what Google and ChatGPT reward)

1. **Answer first.** The `summary` field answers the reader's question in 2–3
   sentences, so an AI assistant can quote it directly.
2. **H2 headings phrased the way people search** — "How to reach Pokhara from
   Gorakhpur", "What does a Nepal trip cost for a family of four?".
3. Short paragraphs (2–4 sentences). Tables for comparisons, costs and
   day-by-day plans. Numbered lists for steps.
4. **Key facts** — 4–6 label/value pairs (best time, duration, budget range,
   visa/ID, how to reach).
5. **FAQ** — 5–8 real questions with direct 1–3 sentence answers.
6. **Sources** — the official or reputable pages the facts came from.
7. 1,400–2,200 words in the body. Long enough to be the best answer, no padding.
8. Link to **2–5 TripGuru pages** from the provided list where they genuinely
   help the reader, and end with a natural reason to contact TripGuru.

## Hard rules — the Checker rejects an article that breaks any of these

- **Never invent a TripGuru price, offer, discount or package.** The only
  TripGuru prices that exist are the ones in the provided page list. For
  anything else say "message us for a quote".
- **Never invent reviews, customer stories, testimonials, awards, years in
  business, number of customers or statistics.**
- Market prices (hotel rates, fares, entry fees, permit fees) only from the
  research, written as approximate ranges with the month and year ("about
  ₹2,500–4,000 a night as of September 2026").
- **Visa, passport, ID, permit and border rules** only from official or
  clearly current sources, dated. Indians need no visa for Nepal — but say
  which ID documents are accepted, and that children's rules differ.
- Nothing that could hurt someone if wrong: no medical advice beyond "consult
  a doctor", no promises about safety, weather or availability.
- Only link to TripGuru pages that are in the provided list. External links
  only to the sources actually used.
- No criticism of competitors or other agencies by name.
- Do not write about political events, protests or disasters as current news.
  If research shows a destination has a live travel advisory, say so plainly
  and link the official advisory.
